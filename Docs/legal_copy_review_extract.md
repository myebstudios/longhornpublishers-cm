## 3. Points Requiring a Client or Legal Ruling

These are raised by the development team from reading the copy against the system's actual behaviour. Items 1–3 are substantive and should go to counsel; items 4–8 are drafting defects the development team can fix immediately once a direction is given.

### Substantive — for legal counsel

**3.1 — No statutory basis is cited.**
Neither document references Cameroonian law by name. Cameroonian counsel should confirm whether the Privacy Policy must cite specific instruments (for example the 2010 cybersecurity and cybercriminality legislation, or any personal-data protection statute currently in force) and whether any registration or declaration obligation attaches to collecting personal data through the site. **The development team cannot answer this and has not attempted to.**

**3.2 — Cross-border data transfer is not disclosed.**
Form submissions are processed by Netlify, a third-party processor whose infrastructure sits outside Cameroon. The current Privacy Policy §3 describes routing to the Yaoundé office but does not disclose that the data crosses a border or names Netlify as a processor. If Cameroonian law requires disclosure of, or consent for, international transfer, §3 is incomplete as written.

**3.3 — No retention period is stated.**
Privacy §3 says data is kept "only as long as necessary" / "uniquement le temps nécessaire." That is a description, not a period. If a defined maximum is required, or simply advisable, the client should specify one (for example 24 or 36 months from last contact) and the development team will wire it in.

### Drafting defects — EN/FR divergence and grammar

**3.4 — The French non-disclosure commitment is narrower than the English.**
English §2: *"We do not sell, rent, or trade your personal information to third parties."* — unconditional.
French §2: *"Nous ne vendons, ne louons et ne partageons vos données personnelles avec aucun tiers à des fins commerciales."* — the trailing "à des fins commerciales" (*for commercial purposes*) qualifies the promise, permitting non-commercial sharing that the English text forbids. **Two language versions of the same policy should not make different promises.** Recommend deleting the qualifier from the French.

**3.5 — Terms §3 headings and scope diverge.**
English: *"Website Content & Educational Materials"* … *"for informational and B2B inquiry purposes."*
French: *"Contenus du site et offres de services"* … *"à titre informatif"* — the B2B inquiry framing is dropped and the heading names a different subject. Recommend aligning both to the English scope.

**3.6 — French grammatical error, Terms §4.**
*"de spams commercial non sollicité"* — number/gender disagreement. Should read *"de spam commercial non sollicité"* (singular) or *"de spams commerciaux non sollicités"* (plural).

**3.7 — French heading, Privacy §5.**
Currently *"Vos droits et contacts."* This is not idiomatic; nor was the previous *"Vos droits et contact."* Recommend *"Vos droits et nos coordonnées."*

**3.8 — The "last updated" date is hardcoded.**
Both documents display "September 2026" / "Septembre 2026" from a fixed string in the translation files. It will not advance on its own. If the client amends the copy after sign-off, the development team must be told to bump this value in the same pass, or it will silently misstate the revision date.
